# Social and ethical issues
This page outlines social/ethical issues that are relevant to the project.

## Maintaining transparency with stakeholders/clients
Open communication with stakeholders should be transparent in order to act in an ethical manner. Information about any progress, challenges, changes, executive decisions and leadership structures (if applicable) should be shared to keep stakeholders adequately informed and build trust. Any communication with clients during this project will follow this principle.

In this project, communication between developers and clients will be as transparent as possible, allowing them to holistically understand and engage with the project. This allows them to provide accurate feedback, but also understand why their feedback may or may not have been implemented into the software solution.

Without transparent communication, trust between developers and the stakeholders (teachers and senior crew members) erodes. If developers are not transparent, it can lead to misunderstandings which can be costly and expensive. It may also cause stakeholders to question the honesty and credibility of the developer. 

This would disrupt the cycle of feedback during the development of this system, causing this project to stray away from its WAgile approach. This project would then lack valuable feedback from a diverse audience, leading to a poorer software solution. More generally, a lack of trust makes a company more "likely [to] lose employees, customers, and the benefit of the doubt of other critical stakeholders".[^1]

## Ensuring Inclusivity
Software should be inclusive of all users, including those with disabilities, by following accessibility guidelines and inclusive coding practices. This means that the software does not exclude people with certain disabilities, such as auditory, physical or visual impairments.

In this project, it is not an essential requirement to support mobile devices and alternative UI options (such as light and dark mode). This is due to time constraints, but it leads to the exclusion of groups, like people who may only be able to use mobile phones or people who are unable to navigate darker interfaces due to their eyesight. From an ethical standpoint, it is a huge issue to exclude certain social groups because everyone should have equal access to the web and its content.[^2]

From a business standpoint, ensuring inclusivity improves the user experience and increases the amount of customers and boosts satisfaction. 

From a legal standpoint, ensuring inclusivity and producing accessible software is required by law in specific cases, such as, when implementing government websites.[^3] This does not apply to the project, so there is no external pressure to comply to web accessibility standards.

Due to the time constraints of the project, there is no guarantee that accessibility features will be built into the software. The following measures will be taken in order to mitigate the exclusion of people with disabilities:
- Semantic HTML will be used, allowing screen readers to describe content more accurately. For example, using `<button>` instead of `<div class="button">`
- Any images will have alternative text
- The colour of text and other elements will follow WCAG level AA contrast requirements
- A short, automated test will be run to check compliance with commonly neglected accessibility guidelines
